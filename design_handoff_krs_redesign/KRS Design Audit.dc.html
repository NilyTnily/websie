5.5

Home

What's wrong
  • The collections grid is grid-cols-3 holding two categories. A permanent empty third cell is the first thing below the fold.
  • Hero title, collections, featured, testimonials and CTA all use the same 36px Playfair centered heading on the same 80px rhythm. Five sections, one voice, no hierarchy.
  • The CTA offers two outline buttons of identical weight. Neither is primary, so neither gets clicked.
  • "Discover Watches →" is repeated under a tile that is already entirely clickable.
  • An autoplaying CGI video with no default poster carries the whole first impression, and the headline sits on top of the busiest part of the frame.
What's right
  • Copy is genuinely good. "Nothing on the shelf is made twice" is worth building a page around.
  • Zero border-radius is a real position, and mostly held.
  • The hairline divider is a nice restrained device most stores would have made a 1px border.
6.0

Collection

  • Two search fields on one screen — one in the header, one above the grid — with different behaviour.
  • The price slider spans $2,200 to $412,000 at step={50}. That is over eight thousand drag positions for one thumb.
  • Seven facet groups render in a flat vertical stack with no collapse, so the sidebar height changes drastically as scope changes. Watch facets (movement, case size, water resistance) sit next to jewelry facets with nothing separating the two worlds.
  • Sort is a bare native <select> — the one element on the page that doesn't obey the design language.
  • The product card nests a wishlist button and an "Add to Bag" button inside the <Link> that wraps the whole card. Interactive elements inside a link is both an a11y problem and a mis-click generator.
  • 96 pieces, no pagination, no grouping by house. A Rolex and a pair of studs get identical cards.
6.5

Product detail

  • The best screen in the build. The specs table and the details list are exactly right for the category.
  • The price is 24px at normal weight — visually equal to the paragraph under it. On a $34,000 piece that number should be the second thing you see.
  • The buy panel scrolls away. Gallery, specs, related pieces and reviews run long below it with no sticky rail to return to.
  • The trust bar — hallmarked, insured, lifetime care — is a small grey list at the bottom of the column. It is the entire argument for buying here rather than at auction.
  • The loupe interaction exists in the CSS and is the single most distinctive thing in the codebase. It appears on one component.
4.0

Cart & checkout

  • There is no checkout route in the app. The bag is a drawer that ends at an inquiry form. For a house that ships insured and signature-required, the most reassuring moment in the journey is missing entirely.
  • No shipping estimate, no duties note, no presentation-case or engraving choice, no gift note — all standard at this price and all margin.
  • The drawer gives no route back to the piece and no cross-sell.
6.0

About / brand story

  • A 16:7 stock hero, two paragraphs, then a four-tile icon grid. That is the shape of every template About page.
  • The writing is the best on the site — "small enough that the person who services your movement is the same person who packed the box" — and it is set at 14px grey.
  • No workshop imagery, no people, no process. The claim is craft; the page shows none of it.
5.0

Account & orders

  • Reads as a generic order list. The site promises "a record of every service and restoration on your collection" in its own CTA and then doesn't deliver one.
  • No certificates, no provenance dossier, no service history, no valuation — the things an owner of these pieces actually returns for.
  • Signed-in navigation swaps the whole main nav out for Profile / My Orders, so the catalog disappears while you are logged in and browsing.
6.5

Admin

  • Nine well-chosen sections and a working editor for homepage copy — more capable than most stores this size. It just looks like stock shadcn.
  • The summary opens on four flat counters. Nothing tells the owner which piece is about to sell out, which inquiry is aging, or what a photo shoot would be worth.

Cross-cutting — the four that matter

Two brands are fighting

The code says navy and brass, explicitly a Harry Winston reference. Your brand book says mocha, tobacco, champagne, ivory, onyx — Italian elegance, Japanese precision. The site is currently dressed as someone else. Everything below follows from fixing this one.

Three typefaces, one job

Playfair, EB Garamond and IBM Plex Mono all run at once, and the uppercase mono treatment is applied to nav, prices, filter labels, spec values and footer heads alike. The loudest typographic device on the site is used for the least important content.

Everything is 80px apart

Every section is py-20 inside max-w-7xl. No full-bleed moment, no change of tempo, no image ever allowed to be large. Luxury retail is built on the contrast between one enormous image and a lot of empty space; this site has neither extreme.

Mobile is a narrowed desktop

The 64px header keeps four icon buttons and hides the entire catalog behind a hamburger. Filters become a left sheet. There is no thumb-reachable primary action anywhere in the flow — and no app at all.

Where this goes next: the brand book palette applied properly, one editorial rhythm instead of five identical bands, the loupe promoted to the signature interaction it was written to be, a real checkout, an owner's vault in the account, and a native shopping app for iOS and Android.
